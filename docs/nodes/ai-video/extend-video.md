# Extend Video

> Continue a generated video with a new prompt direction.

## Overview

The Extend Video node continues a video by appending new content guided by a text prompt. VEO and Runway extends require an upstream video generated by the matching node (they need the original provider task ID), and so does Grok: it extends only a Grok Imagine video you made here. LTX 2.3 Pro, Seedance 2.5, Seedance 2.0, Kling 4 and PixVerse V6 work on **any** video URL — including uploads. Use it to create longer videos from short clips.

Seedance comes as two choices, one per model: **Seedance 2.5** (`seedance-2-5-extend`, adds up to 30 seconds per run) and **Seedance 2.0** (`seedance-2-0-extend`, up to 15 seconds). **Kling 4** (`kling-4-extend`) adds 3 to 30 seconds, at 720p or 1080p. **PixVerse V6** (`pixverse-v6-extend`) adds 1 to 15 seconds, at 360p, 540p, 720p or 1080p, with or without sound; only its new seconds are joined onto your original video (see [How PixVerse V6 extend works](#how-pixverse-v6-extend-works)). **Grok** (`grok-extend`) adds 6 or 10 seconds to a Grok Imagine video you made here, at that video's own resolution (see [How Grok extend works](#how-grok-extend-works)). They continue a clip in different ways and are priced differently (see [How Seedance and Kling 4 extend work](#how-seedance-and-kling-4-extend-work) and the credit sections below).

> **Free credits:** until an account buys anything, extending a video is not available, on any provider — see [Free credits](../../features/free-credits.md#video-on-free-credits).

## Inputs & Outputs

**Inputs:**
- Video (required) — upstream VEO, Runway, or LTX 2.3 Pro generated video. VEO and Runway extends require the original provider task ID (so the source must come from the corresponding generation node); so does Grok, whose source must be a Grok Imagine node (text-to-video or image-to-video) that ran in your account here. LTX 2.3 Pro, Seedance 2.5, Seedance 2.0, Kling 4 and PixVerse V6 accept any video URL.

**Outputs:**
- Extended video URL

LTX 2.3 Fast does **not** support extend mode — only Pro extends. The `extend_mode` field controls whether the new footage is prepended (`start`) or appended (`end`, default).

For Seedance 2.5, Seedance 2.0, Kling 4 and PixVerse V6, the resolution is the one the new footage is generated at, and the one the run is priced by. Whichever you pick, the new footage is scaled to the source's frame, up from a lower resolution or down from a higher one. The video you get back keeps the source's size and frame rate, and is encoded once as an H.264 MP4 with AAC sound: an H.264 source at its own bitrate (or higher, when its own is very low for its size and frame rate), and a source in any other codec, or an H.264 source that reports no bitrate, at a high constant quality. A 10-bit, 4:2:2 or 4:4:4 source comes back as 8-bit 4:2:0, an HDR source in standard dynamic range (SDR), and a full-range source in standard range (a watermarked run is encoded once more; see [How Seedance and Kling 4 extend work](#how-seedance-and-kling-4-extend-work)). A source with an odd width or height comes back at the even size just below it, its last column of pixels, last row, or both cropped (853×481 comes back 852×480), because the delivered video's 4:2:0 colour needs even sides.

An extend that names the earlier single Seedance id, `seedance-2-extend` (from before Seedance 2.5 and Seedance 2.0 were separate choices), runs on **Seedance 2.5**. Saved workflows, apps and API or MCP calls on that id keep working, and now run, and are priced, as Seedance 2.5 (a length under 4 seconds, which the earlier id raised to 4, still runs as 4 seconds rather than being refused) — 8 seconds added at 720p to a source of 10 seconds or longer, for example, is 1881 credits<!-- credits: seedance-2-5-extend:720p:per-2s@9 --> on Seedance 2.5 and 688 credits<!-- credits: seedance-2-0-extend:8s:720p --> on Seedance 2.0. Choose Seedance 2.0 to keep that model.

### Lengths and sources a run refuses

These are refused with HTTP 400 when the request arrives, before any credits are reserved:

- **A length outside the provider's window** (`validation_error`): Seedance 2.5 adds 4–30 seconds, Seedance 2.0 4–15, Kling 4 3–30, PixVerse V6 1–15, Grok 6 or 10, LTX 2.3 Pro 1–20, always a whole number of seconds. VEO and Runway add a length of their own; a `duration` sent to them must be 1–20 and does not change it.
- **A resolution the model does not render** (`validation_error`): Kling 4 renders 720p or 1080p, so a `resolution` of 480p sent to `kling-4-extend` is refused. (A saved node or workflow carrying 480p onto Kling 4 runs at 720p, the resolution it is priced at.) PixVerse V6 renders 360p, 540p, 720p or 1080p, so 480p sent to `pixverse-v6-extend` is refused, and so is 360p or 540p sent to Seedance or Kling 4. (A saved node or workflow carrying 480p onto PixVerse V6 runs at 540p, the nearest quality it renders, and is priced at it.)
- **A prompt under 3 characters** (`validation_error`, PixVerse V6): it needs at least 3 characters describing what happens next.
- **A source Grok cannot extend** (`grok_extend_source`, Grok): anything but a finished Grok Imagine video made from text or an image, at 480p or 720p, in your account here — an uploaded video, another model's video, another user's, a video Grok Extend made, or a request that names no source task. The error reads: "Grok can only extend a finished Grok Imagine video (made from text or an image, at 480p or 720p) that you created here. It can't extend uploaded videos, videos from other models (including Grok Imagine Video 1.5), or other people's videos. To extend any other video, choose another Extend provider."
- **A source too short to continue** (`video_too_short`, Seedance and Kling 4): each continues from the last seconds of the source's picture (up to 10 seconds on Seedance 2.5, 2 on Seedance 2.0, 3 on Kling 4, or all of a shorter source). Seedance needs at least 1.8 seconds of it, Kling 4 at least 2 seconds. Kling 4 continues a source shorter than 3 seconds at the same price.

On Seedance 2.5, Seedance 2.0, Kling 4, PixVerse V6 and Grok, a run can also fail after the new footage is generated: when the model returns less than half the seconds the run is priced for (under 2.5 seconds of a 5-second extension, say), the run fails and every credit it reserved is refunded. You never get back the source alone, or the source with the short extension joined on; the job's error says how many seconds the model returned. On PixVerse V6 and Grok the new seconds are what it returns beyond the source's length, so a run where it returns only the source fails the same way.

### How Seedance and Kling 4 extend work

Seedance 2.5, Seedance 2.0 and Kling 4 generate the continuation natively (the model watches the end of the source clip and produces only what happens next, soundtrack included), then the platform stitches source + extension into one clip. They are asked differently:

- **Seedance 2.5** sends the model the **last 10 seconds** of the source's picture (the whole source, when it is shorter) as `@video_1`, **declared as an extension** (the request's reference task type is `extend`), with **no start image**, and the prompt `Extend @video_1 forward by <seconds> seconds: <your prompt>`. The model continues the clip it watched with new footage only: its first frame follows the source's last, it does not repeat it. So nothing at the join is a duplicate, and the stitch is a **hard cut at the source's end** with a short audio blend; no frame of either clip is dropped. The extension runs at the source's own aspect ratio (`adaptive`). When the source is itself a Seedance 2.5 extension kept as a MOV (`KIE_SEEDANCE_25_OUTPUT_FORMAT=mov`), the seconds sent are cut from that MOV when it holds all of them, and from the delivered video otherwise.
- **Seedance 2.0** and **Kling 4** send the source's **last 2 seconds** (Kling 4: its last 3 seconds; all of a shorter source) as `@video_1` and the source's **last frame** as the opening frame of the extension, then **smart-stitch**: it PSNR-matches the source's last 8 frames against the extension's first 8, ends the source **on** the most similar frame and starts the extension **right after** its near-identical twin — the duplicated boundary moment plays exactly once and motion continues through the cut. If no genuine match is found (below the 24dB threshold), the stitch falls back to fixed trims (source's last 4 frames + extension's first 3).

On all three, the join is a hard cut with a short audio blend, and the applied cut is reported in the job's `output_data.smartCuts` (frames dropped each side + match PSNR; on Seedance 2.5 nothing is dropped and no match is searched for). The extension is scaled to cover the source's frame and centre-cropped to it. The video you get back keeps the source's size and frame rate, and is encoded once as an H.264 MP4 with AAC sound: an H.264 source at its own bitrate (or higher, when its own is very low for its size and frame rate), and a source in any other codec, or an H.264 source that reports no bitrate, at a high constant quality. A 10-bit, 4:2:2 or 4:4:4 source comes back as 8-bit 4:2:0, an HDR source in standard dynamic range (SDR), and a full-range source in standard range. A source with an odd width or height comes back at the even size just below it, its last column of pixels, last row, or both cropped (853×481 comes back 852×480), because the delivered video's 4:2:0 colour needs even sides. A run that carries the free-tier watermark is encoded once more to add it: its size (rounded down to even) and frame rate stay the source's, its picture quality does not.

### How PixVerse V6 extend works

PixVerse V6 extends natively: the whole source video goes to PixVerse, which returns the same video with the new seconds joined on. The video you get back keeps your original footage, never PixVerse's re-render of it. The platform cuts PixVerse's new seconds from its video at the source's length, measured in seconds of picture (PixVerse may return the video at another frame rate), and joins them to your original video with the same stitch as the other extends, frame matching included: the source's last 8 frames are PSNR-matched against the new seconds' first 8, and when no genuine match is found the join is left as it is (there are no fixed trims). Either way, if the new seconds then open on a brief hold, the frames at their start that repeat the new frame before them (a PSNR of 50dB or more) are dropped, at most 12. The join is a hard cut with a short audio blend; the applied cut is reported in the job's `output_data.smartCuts`, its `nextClipStartTrimFrames` counting any dropped repeats. The new seconds are scaled to cover the source's frame and centre-cropped to it, and the result keeps the source's size (rounded down to even), frame rate and quality, encoded once as an H.264 MP4 with AAC sound, by the same rules as in [How Seedance and Kling 4 extend work](#how-seedance-and-kling-4-extend-work). With the sound switch off, the new seconds are silent. Your prompt is sent with no continuation wording wrapped around it, so describe only what happens next.

### How Grok extend works

Grok extends only its own videos: a finished Grok Imagine video made from text or an image (the `grok` and `grok-i2v` models), at 480p or 720p, made in your account on this platform. Like VEO's and Runway's extends, the node takes the task of the upstream Grok Imagine node that made the video (`taskId` on the API, `task_id` on MCP), never the video's URL. Any other source is refused with `grok_extend_source` before any credits are reserved (see [Lengths and sources a run refuses](#lengths-and-sources-a-run-refuses)); that includes a video Grok Extend made, which another Extend provider can continue by its URL.

Grok continues from the end of the source's picture (measured to the hundredth of a second) and returns the whole video: its own render of the source, then the new seconds. That video is what you get back, as Grok returns it: there is no stitch, and it is re-encoded only to add the free-tier watermark, or when its format is not one browsers play. The new seconds are generated at the source's resolution and always have sound. Your prompt is sent with no continuation wording wrapped around it, so describe only what happens next.

### Reference images (Seedance only)

`POST /v1/extend-video` and the MCP `extend_video` tool accept reference images
alongside the continuation prompt, so the extension can hold a specific
identity, product, or object steady — instead of describing it by name and
hoping:

- **`referenceImageUrls`** — a flat URL list. Mention each as `@image_1` …
  `@image_N` (list order) in the prompt.
- **`connectedReferences`** — the same structured-reference shape
  `/v1/generate-video` takes (see
  [Structured references on video](../../api-integration.md#structured-references-connectedreferences-on-video)):
  entity references assemble server-side into attached images + per-reference
  directives.

**Up to 8 user references attach**, on both Seedance providers — the Seedance
2.0 image cap minus one, because Seedance 2.0's extension itself occupies one
reference seat with the source's last frame (the continuation anchor); Seedance
2.5 sends no anchor and takes the same 8, so a node's prompt and references stay
the same on either model. User references are numbered `@image_1…@image_8`
(on Seedance 2.0, **ahead** of the anchor, so their ordinals never shift); the
source's end rides as `@video_1` (its last 10 seconds on Seedance 2.5, its last
2 seconds on Seedance 2.0).

Only the Seedance providers (`seedance-2-5-extend`, `seedance-2-0-extend`) have
a reference path. Sending reference images with any other extend provider,
Kling 4, PixVerse V6 and Grok included, returns a 400 (they would otherwise be silently ignored). Reference images add
**no extra credits**.

### Seedance 2.5 extend credits

Charged per second of video the run sends and adds: the seconds of the source it sends (its last 10 seconds, or all of a shorter source, measured on its picture) **plus** the seconds added, at the resolution the new footage is generated at (the delivered video keeps the source's size, rounded down to even):

| Resolution | Per second sent or added |
|---|---|
| 480p | 46.75<!-- credits-rate: seedance-2-5-extend:480p:per-2s@0.5 --> |
| 720p | 104.5<!-- credits-rate: seedance-2-5-extend:720p:per-2s@0.5 --> |
| 1080p | 261.25<!-- credits-rate: seedance-2-5-extend:1080p:per-2s@0.5 --> |

```
seconds sent = min(10, the source's length in seconds)
credits      = (seconds sent + seconds added) × the per-second price, rounded up to a whole credit
```

The charge itself is worked out from the price of two seconds: that price × (seconds sent + seconds added) ÷ 2 is rounded up to a whole credit before the deployment's pricing settings are applied, and once more after, so a run can come to 1 credit<!-- credits: n/a --> more than the formula above gives, never more.

## Best Practices

- For VEO / Runway: the source video must come from the matching generation node — this node cannot extend arbitrary uploaded videos for those providers.
- For Grok: connect the Grok Imagine node (text-to-video or image-to-video) whose video you want to continue, and describe only what happens next. To continue an uploaded video, another model's video or a Grok extension, choose another provider.
- For LTX 2.3 Pro: any video URL works; use `extend_mode: "start"` to add a lead-in, `extend_mode: "end"` (default) to add a continuation.
- For Seedance 2.5, Seedance 2.0, Kling 4 and PixVerse V6: describe only what happens next, in plain action language (e.g. "the ball keeps rolling until it hits a cup") — on Seedance and Kling 4 the platform handles the continuation phrasing; PixVerse V6 gets your prompt with no continuation wording around it, and needs at least 3 characters. Seedance 2.5 watches up to the last 10 seconds of the source, so it can bring back a person or object seen earlier in that span; it may also open the extension on a different framing of the same scene. Pick the source's resolution for the cleanest seam: the new footage is scaled to the source's size, so a lower resolution looks softer after the join, and a higher one is scaled down to the source's size.
- For Seedance with reference images: mention every reference you attach (`@image_1`, `@image_2`, …) in the continuation prompt — an unmentioned flat reference still rides along, but naming it tells the model what it's for.
- Write prompts that describe the continuation, not the original content.
- Use "fast" mode for iteration, "quality" for final output (VEO).
- Chain multiple Extend Video nodes to create progressively longer content — each LTX 2.3 Pro extend adds up to 20s, each Seedance 2.5 or Kling 4 extend up to 30s and each PixVerse V6 extend up to 15s, so two chained runs give +40s, +60s or +30s.

## Common Use Cases

- Extend a 5-second VEO clip into a 15+ second video.
- Continue a scene with a new action or direction change.
- Build longer narratives by chaining extensions.
- Prepend an establishing shot in front of a hero clip (LTX 2.3 Pro with `extend_mode: "start"`).
- Create variations of how a scene continues.

## Tips

- Use "fast" mode for draft iterations and "quality" for final renders (VEO).
- The prompt for extension should describe what happens next (or, for `start` mode, what happens before).
- Each VEO/Runway extension adds roughly the same duration as the original clip; LTX 2.3 Pro, Seedance, Kling 4 and PixVerse V6 extensions add exactly the user-specified duration (up to +20s per run on LTX 2.3 Pro, +30s on Seedance 2.5, +15s on Seedance 2.0, +30s on Kling 4, +15s on PixVerse V6); Grok adds +6s or +10s.
